# Chinese illustrated Tarot — vetted sources and integration policy

## Preferred deck: The Chinese Tarot by Jui Guoliang

- Artist: **Jui Guoliang (瑞国良; transliterations vary)**. Publication circa 1989, U.S. Games Systems. Complete 78 fully illustrated ink-and-watercolor images, Chinese-inspired motifs and card naming.
- Official publisher and sample artwork: https://www.usgamesinc.com/the-chinese-tarot.html
- Multiple genuine photographs of the cards (reference for visual review, **not a grant to redistribute**): https://benebellwen.com/2021/06/07/the-chinese-tarot-by-jui-guoliang/
- Additional reference: https://www.learntarot.com/chdesc.htm
- Permissions form: https://www.usgamesinc.com/image-reproduction-and-usage.html
- Reproduction policy: https://www.usgamesinc.com/info/TarotReproductionPolicies.pdf
- Permission contact: permissions@usgamesinc.com; publisher reports 2–4 weeks for general requests.

U.S. Games Systems explicitly covers **electronic reproductions**, including internet/app display. Buying a personal deck or locating digital scans does **not** give this public GitHub Pages application the right to distribute all 78 images. The public app therefore **must not scrape, download or embed this deck** prior to license confirmation. Artwork approval by the product owner is not a publisher license.

## Available pathway for private previews

The browser supports importing a complete **user-supplied, privately authorized collection** for the current tab session. No images are sent to the site, GitHub, AI providers or a server; only `blob:` object URLs are used and revoked on re-import/page unload.

- Select **Decks → Download 78-card filename guide** to find the naming convention (`major-00.jpg`, `wands-01.jpg` etc.).
- Prepare exactly 78 individual JPEG/PNG/WebP files, one per ID. The app rejects duplicate or unmapped files and files larger than 12 MiB.
- Under **Decks → Private local preview** select those 78 files together. The gallery, reveal and reading preview will use the local images until refresh. The interpretation is still based on standard Tarot correspondence; it does not purport to reproduce Jui Guoliang's copyrighted booklet.
- Digital files should be obtained through authorized channels and used in accordance with the rights that actually apply. The feature is **not** a claim that all private copying is universally lawful.
- The AI backend is disabled for the private imported deck until its metadata/rights mapping is cleared.

## Future public Chinese deck choices

1. Request a full digital-use license from U.S. Games Systems covering 78 images on a public website, mobile app and all target territories, and clarify allowed card-frame overlays/shine. Their reproduction policy says copyrighted card images may not be altered without permission.
2. If declined/unaffordable, obtain **another complete 78-card Chinese-themed illustrated deck with an explicit reuse license**, or commission original illustrated artwork. Verify the actual source/artist/license and all 78 visually distinct images. Never fill missing images with vectors, placeholders or simplified icons.
3. A Chinese **text** translation of RWS is not a new Chinese **illustration deck**. Keep language, cultural reading perspective, and illustration choice independently selectable.

No complete Chinese-inspired Tarot set with a verified redistribution-compatible license was confirmed in the research as of November 2026. Public-domain historical Asian art pieces could inspire an original deck but do not automatically form an authentic, coherent 78-card Tarot deck.
